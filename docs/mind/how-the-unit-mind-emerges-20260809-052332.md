**Original Question:**  
This sounds reasonable, mahodaya. Since the unit ahamtattva has no tamas, it could not have come from adding qualities to the cosmic chitta, which has tamas. The cosmic chitta should have been made more subtle to yield the unit ahamtattva. 



I guess we are revising our earlier model of unit mahattattva arising from unit consciousnesses and unit ahamtattva arising from unit mahattattva. That sequence actually happens for the cosmic mind. 



For the unit mind, a part of the cosmic chitta becomes the unit chitta, a part of the unit chitta becomes subtler to become the unit ahamtattva, and a part of the latter becomes subtler to become the unit mahattattva. This unit mahattattva reflects the cosmic consciousness, and that reflection is the unit mind. 



The above model can explain more complex structures than the previous simpler model. Is the above right?



**Full Answer:**  
Re your second paragraph-- yes true. But we are not doing this revision now: I have explained earlier that the explanation provided in the first karapatram is a heuristic device only, albeit an extremely useful one. I thought I had already told about that in this WA group, haven't I?



Re your third paragraph: It is not that "a part of the unit chitta becomes subtler to become the unit ahamtattva", as there is no process of becoming here regarding mind. The three parts of mind were created from 1:00 to 3:00 and are not made at any other point in the srsticakra. What happens at 6:00 is that in the process of moving to 6:01, the tristariiya man emerges from ksititattva. It does not "become" mind; rather, mind is already there-- and it is there as tristariiya man (although not overtly manifest so long as it is externally paincabhuta). So it is a process of emergence rather than of becoming, and this is an important distinction.
