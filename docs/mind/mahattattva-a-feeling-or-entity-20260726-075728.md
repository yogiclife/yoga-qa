**Original Question:**  
In explaining paragraph 2, and at many other times, it is mentioned that the feeling of I is mahattattva. I have generally been thinking of mahattattva as the entity that generates a feeling of I (apart from its role in reflecting the infinite consciousness), rather than the feeling itself. A feeling appears to be some type of knowledge or experience, while the mahattattva is an actual substance/vastu -- purusha. It is not clear to me how a feeling would be a substance. Can you clarify whether we really mean mahattattva to be the feeling of I, or whether it is the entity that produces the feeling? Also, having produced the feeling, I assume it is the ahamtattva that actually gets the knowledge "I exist".



**Full Answer:**  
Mahattattva *is* the "I'-feeling, Ahamtattva is the "doer I", and citta is the "done I".



Better not to try to integrate yoga with nyaya shastra.
