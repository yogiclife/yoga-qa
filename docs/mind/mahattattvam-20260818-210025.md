**Original Question:**  
Mahattattvam

**Full Answer:**  
The mahattattva is the part of the mind which gives you the feeling that you exist. When you sit in a room, you know you are there, and the reason is that you are aware of your own existence in this world. Cats and dogs do not have this feeling. And this feeling, the "I-feeling", adds a huge dimension to our functionality as human beings. Both in the relative world of this universe, as well as in our yearning to know the Infinite. It is like taking a computer chip from 1990 and comparing it to a computer chip from 2023. The mahattattva gives you that sort of orders of magnitude increase in functionality-- both in the material world and in the supra-mental or spiritual world i.e. the world of purusha or consciousness. I would just add that the addition of mahattattva, i.e. an active mahattattva, in the mind brings not only a quantitative change but a qualitative change-- this is vitally important.Just reviewed your letter, and saw the example you'd given. In your sentence, ‘I enjoy eating ice cream’, the 'I' is ahamtattva. Not mahattattva. The mahattattva is "I -feeling", whereas the ahamtattva is "doer-I". The mahattattva does not do anything. Everything you do-- all the things you experience as well as all the acts you perform-- you do all this with your ahamtattva. But you are only able to do the actions as the performer of the action, as well as to experience the sensations as an aware experiencer (i.e not only do you see the tree, that much a cat or dog does, but in addition you know you are seeing the tree), because of the qualitative dimension added to your mental functioning by mahattattva.
