**Original Question:**  
I am trying to understand mahattattvam, mahodaya. Is it ‘I’ in a limited sense, when the mind identifies the self with the material existence? And atma is our true self? In that sense, it is reasonable for the mind to think that ‘I enjoy eating ice cream’. The atma would not enjoy ice cream. So, ‘I’ is used in the sense of the mahattatvam in the above sentence.

**Full Answer:**  
The mahattattva is the part of the mind which gives you the feeling that you exist. When you sit in a room, you know you are there, and the reason is that you are aware of your own existence in this world. Cats and dogs do not have this feeling. And this feeling, the "I-feeling", adds a huge dimension to our functionality as human beings. Both in the relative world of this universe, as well as in our yearning to know the Infinite. It is like taking a computer chip from 1990 and comparing it to a computer chip from 2023. The mahattattva gives you that sort of orders of magnitude increase in functionality-- both in the material world and in the supra-mental or spiritual world i.e. the world of purusha or consciousness.
