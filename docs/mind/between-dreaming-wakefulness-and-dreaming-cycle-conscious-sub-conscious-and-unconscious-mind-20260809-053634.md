**Original Question:**  
Mahodaya, one student wanted to know how we can distinguish between reality and imagination. Let us say we are cycling between dreaming and wakefulness and dreaming again. How do we know which one was a dream and which one was real?



**Full Answer:**  
Good question, and yes I listened to the whole discussion about this in your session. One thing I'd like to emphasize about this at the outset is that, while this question may seem like a difficult subject to analyze intellectually, in real life no one is confused about it. Have you ever seen someone come and ask with help in distinguishing between reality and dream? Unlike what Sandeep ji was suggesting, people do not need to do longitudinal analyses through time to test which world is consistent. They know very well from birth what reality is; it is not even something they need to learn. The reason behind this is that the mind has three components: (1) the conscious mind, (2) the subconscious mind, and (3) the unconscious mind. It is the duty of the conscious mind to distinguish between one's inner thoughts and the external world. And it is fully adept at making this distinction; it does not need to undertake any analysis or testing. Observing for what is consistent through time is not at all needed. The conscious mind is simply aware of what the external objective world is and what its inner thoughts are. The conscious mind is however only functional while one is awake. When one goes to sleep the conscious mind is not active; it goes into a dormant state, and the subconscious mind takes over running the ship. The subconcious mind is the component of mind which dreams, and it has no awareness of the difference between the external objective reality and the dream world. That is why when one is asleep the dream seems real. Yet the moment one awakens in the morning one realizes immediately that it was all a dream; the reason for this is that the conscious mind becomes active and immediately makes the distinction. The unconscious mind is the part of the mind that controls respiration and other basic functions; one does not need to "think" about these things; they are being continuously monitored and regulated by the unconscious mind. 



The last thing to mention here is the extensive discussion you both had together during the session about where in the awake state it may even become unclear to the mind whether something is a dream or not. You were talking about how the mind usually rapidly shifts or toggles between imagination and external reality and because of this does not lose track of what is imagination and what is external reality. And that is indeed true; another way of describing this is to say that during such states of imagination the conscious mind is active and providing continuous input informing that this is just imagination. Where that may change is when for example when someone is hypnotized by a hypnotist; or when someone goes into a very deep state of imagination on their own. It can happen in quite normal circumstances, such as if a high school student is sitting in class but looking out the window, and their mind wonders deeply into some thoughts such that when the teacher calls on them they do not even hear; their conscious mind has gone into a dormant state. The same occurs when someone goes to a hypnotist. Normally the conscious mind is fully active while we are awake and hence there is no question of confusion or losing track of what is reality. But in certain circumstances as described above it can happen, due to which even while awake imagination can seem real. 



When one is awake, all three components of the mind are active; during sleep the conscious mind becomes dormant, such that during dream stages of sleep the subconscious mind is dominant, and when the mind goes into deep sleep where there is no dreaming, there the subconscious mind also becomes dormant and the unconscious mind is the only component of mind that remains active.
