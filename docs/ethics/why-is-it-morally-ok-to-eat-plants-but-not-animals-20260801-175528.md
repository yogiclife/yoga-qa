**Original Question:**  
Clarification by acharya ji.

**Full Answer:**  
4. Someone asked if we are to stop meat, fish, eggs, and dairy in order to avoid causing harm to others, then why should we eat plants? They are also living beings.

- The first step of the reply to this question is: jiivaH jiivasya bhojanam. Every living being has to eat other living beings in order to survive. We cannot live by eating stones. And we have a duty to maintain our own life-- indeed not to do so goes against a fundamental principle of the universe. So we have to eat something, and that something has to be a living being.

- And when we have to eat something, then we should eat the lowest on the scale of development as possible. This is our duty, in order to cause the least possible suffering to others in order to maintain our existence. This is the principle of ahimsa. If we eat a fish when we could have eaten some beans and rice, then we are unnecessarily causing suffering to the fish, whereas whatever suffering has been caused to the plant that produced the rice and the bean is far less. Rather than saying that plants cannot experience pain or suffering, it is better to say that their ability to experience pain is far less then a fish, a chicken, a cow, or a goat. And so the principle of ahimsa guides that we are to eat plants rather than animals, and by so doing we cause far less suffering. Indeed this is the guideline of Parama Purusa.
