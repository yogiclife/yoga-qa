**Original Question:**  
Clarification by acharya ji.

**Full Answer:**  
If you've been listening to Mahodaya's classes, then you'll know that two weeks ago in his class one sister who was attending for the first time (up to then she'd been listening to the recordings), upon hearing X Mahodaya explaining about the free will which human beings have, asked him to say something about what is given in the Giita regarding nimittamatra. By the nature of her question it was apparent that she was understanding the term nimittamatra to indicate that humans do not have free will and are just the tools of the Supreme Entity. X ji responded very nicely saying that he'd be keeping for now to the subject at hand and that the reply to her question would be addressed in due course in future classes. So we communicated about it after the session, and I wrote the below to him about nimittamatra.



Namaskar Mahodaya,



What you note here about not discussing other philosophies is well said. Indeed it takes time from our subject, which is yoga-nistha-jiivanam. It is for this reason that I guided Rajendra ji in this way as you note, when he used to ask these questions. 



The question of nimittamatra is different though, because it is indeed a subject in yoga. As we know, human beings have free will. They can follow manava dharma, or they can abuse pashu dharma. Or to phrase it another way, humans can make the desire of Parama Purusa their desire, or they can go on pursuing their own petty selfish desires. If they make the desire of Parama Purusa their own desire, if they want what He wants, if they mould their life so as to become His tool for executing His work of spreading manava dharma throughout the universe, then they become His nimittamatra. 



So it is not that all human beings are His nimittamatra. Someone who is engaged in all manner of crude, negative, degenerate works is not His nimittamatra. Far from it. So if that sister was thinking that by virtue of being a human being we are all the nimittamatra of PP, that is a mistaken conception. We are only His nimittamatra if we merge our desire with His desire so that the two become one. And in that condition, everything we do is His desire only. An elevated sadhaka cannot do anything which is not the desire of Parama Purusa; it is no longer in his blood to be able to even conceive of doing such thing. His every action is a living manifestation of manava dharma. 



So as you were teaching the point of free-will of human beings, the point of nimittamatra is indeed quite relevant. It is not a separate philosophy as X Mahodaya was asking that day, but rather something we discuss in yoga in detail. Human beings, due to their free-will, have the option to make themselves the nimittamatra of Parama Purusa or not. They can abuse pashu dharma and go far, far from the desire of Parama Purusa, or they can become completely dedicated to fulfilling the desire of Parama Purusa in everything they do. It is a matter of making the desire of Parama Purusa my desire. Once the two desires become one, then a sadhaka becomes the nimittamatra of Parama Purusa-- such sadhakas become the hands of Parama Purusa. 



On another note, your handling of the question was perfect-- there was no problem at all. You were respectful of the sister, and at the same time you explained that we will be staying on the subject of what we are discussing, and that the subject she was inquiring about would be covered going forward. 



Especially, as it was not completely clear for you at the moment what she was asking about, and it seemed like it may be off-topic. For this reason only I thought it may be helpful to explain it. That way, when at some point the context comes again, you may explain it if you wish, for it is a subject many are confused about. 



People think nimittamatra means that humans do not have free will and are merely tools of God. That was the sense in which Bhagini asked the question, as she was thinking that "X ji is saying humans have free will but the Gita teaches with nimittamatra that people do not have free will."  But we know that humans are not mere puppets, beings who do not have free-will. If that were the case, then it would be wrong for either Prakrti or indeed the judicial branch of government to punish people for their wrong actions. But if someone steals, they did so knowingly and that should be punishable by law. Some people in society are criminals, and others are sadhus. But not all are nimittamatra-- only the sadhus are -- those who merge their desire with that of Parama Purusa, so that the two desires become one.
