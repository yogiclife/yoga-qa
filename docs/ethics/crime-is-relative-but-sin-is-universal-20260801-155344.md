**Original Question:**  
Clarification by acharya ji.

**Full Answer:**  
1. The first is regarding a question that the new sister X ji asked at the very end of class. It was a very good question. She asked, "You are talking about thoughts being punishable by Prakrti as they are mental actions and so will also have a reaction. But people are brought up in different cultures having different value systems, and so whether a thought is 'good' or 'bad' will be relative to the values of the society in which they live." Her question was about the thoughts a person has, but the question is equally applicable to thoughts (i.e. mental actions) and worldly actions (i.e. those which manifest in the world), as both types of actions will have a reaction brought about by Prakrti, and both will be influenced by the value system of the society in which a person lives. You responded by saying while it is true that there are lots of different samskrtis or cultures in the world, the mind of a person can only go up or down. The reply you gave is true; I would like to just put the reply in a slightly expanded context to make it more clear. The implication of what you said by "the mind of a person can only go up or down" is that there is something universal that transcends societal values such that the thoughts of any person anywhere will experience a reaction to their thoughts that is beyond the relativity of their community values. And this is very true. Here below is the main idea:



- Crime vs sin. Crime is relative, determined by society's values. Papa is universal and transcends any given society's values. 



For example, 230 years ago in the USA, it was socially normal and acceptable behavior to keep slaves. So at that time, in that society, keeping a slave was not a crime. But, although societal  customs were crude and so they did not consider keeping slaves as bad, but it was papa. Papa is independent of time, place, and person. Society's values may be crude, but Prakrti does not judge on the basis of societal values of the age; if any action is harming others then it is papa and there will be samskaras generated in accordance with this universal law. Thomas Jefferson and George Washington both kept slaves. Jefferson owned over 600 people during his lifetime, making him one of the largest slaveholders among the U.S. presidents. According to US custom and law these two men were not criminals. But according to yogic teaching they harmed innumerable people and so they were papiis. And regardless of societal values of the day, they got samskara and karmaphala in accordance with the harm that they did to those human beings. The definition of punya (paropakAra) and papa (parApakAra) does not change in according to the age or social custom. But the definition of crime always changes.



Another example is that when Muslim terrorists go to a public place and blow themselves up, killing themselves and large numbers of people around them, they think it is a virtuous act and they will go to heaven for it. But, although that may be the teaching of their cult, yet what they have done is papa and they will get samskara and karmaphala in accordance with the papa they have committed. So crime is relative, determined by society's values, but punya and papa are universal and transcend any given society's values. 



So to answer X ji's question, the action a person takes, whether mental action or worldly action, will have a reaction that is determined by whether its effect is that of paropakAra or parApakAra, and not by society's values of the time.
