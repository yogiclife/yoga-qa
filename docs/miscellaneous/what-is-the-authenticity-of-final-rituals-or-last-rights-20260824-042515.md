**Original Question:**  
What is the authenticity of the Hindu ritual of giving food to the dead?

**Full Answer:**  
One brother, I think Sandeep ji, asked a question about the Hindu ritual of giving food to the dead. It was a wonderful question, and he asked with an open mind, eager to learn from you. This is a special opportunity as he is seeking your guidance. All three of them have a lot of respect for you, and when they ask a question like this they are genuinely wanting to know what the rational approach is. And I do understand that there is a sensitive aspect to this, as they have been doing these rituals for the dead for the their entire lives. Yet he was not asking the question with the goal of defending the practice; rather, he openly asked whether it was correct or whether it was wrong and just inherited from the elders. And the motive behind his question was that, on the basis of what he has learned so far in the class, the practice of feeding the dead or offering food to them in a ritual is not making any sense.



So there is a way to reply here in which you give guidance and yet do not make it sound like you are being disrespectful as that is not at all our approach.



In reply you said that there are different philosophies and in vedanta there is an explanation for this ritual; in yoga it is not there but it is there in vedanta. The concern I have with this reply is that it makes it sound like all the different philosophical explanations are legitimate in their own right, and among those the yoga darshana is just one. And it is true that we are not here to engage in discussion of comparative philosophies. But you have reached a stage in the class in which the students now have a base upon which to think about these things in a rational way. And indeed due to this rational base, the question arose in Sandeep ji's mind. So at moments such as these I would encourage you to not refrain wholly from giving any guidance. Because if we do this, then it looks like there are hundreds of explanations for the universe and all the stories are fine. Christianity says God created the world in seven days and that is also one way to see the universe; Islam has their creation story which includes all sorts of angels etc and that is also fine; vedanta has its vague sort of explanation which allows for continuing all the religious Hindi rituals and that is also fine. Whereas we know that yoga is the only approach which is rational and which indeed works with modern science. So there is a way of replying to his question which indicates this. We have reached a point in the class where you can give a reply to him on the basis of what we know from yoga. And that is the truth also--it is not that all those religious replies are also fine; to indicate this would be misleading. 



When we know that after death there is the bodiless mind which is awaiting assignment to a new body, that means that this bodiless mind cannot eat anything and indeed has no need for food whatsoever. The question does not arise of its eating anything. After leaving the body they do not need food. They become a bodiless mind, and in that state, i.e. as soon as they leave their body, there is no scope for us to care for them and nor do we have any responsibility to do so. They are under the care of Prakrti, who will find them a new body if they have not gotten mukti-moksha (which they would not have gotten if they had not done yoga-sadhana in their life). And furthermore we have no idea when they will get a new body; at the time of death they may have immediately been implanted into a newly conceived fetus on this planet or some other planet. And so they are in the womb of a mother somewhere. And we are doing rituals and offering them food. It makes zero sense. So without telling them outright that such things are foolish, you can softly prepare the pathway for them to make the conclusion themselves on the basis of what they have learned. This is part of our viprocita seva to them.
