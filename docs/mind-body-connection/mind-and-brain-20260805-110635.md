**Original Question:**  
We studied that the mind cannot function without the brain. Does this refer to the ahamtattava and chitta alone? Would the mahattattva still be generating the feeling of "I"? During your class on the the karapatram that we are currently studying, someone had raised a question on brain death. You mentioned that the mind and pranas continue being in the body even beyond brain death when the person is on life support. I was wondering if you can share thoughts on the ethics of turning of life support in that situation.

[10/30/25, 11:08:21 PM] Ashok: ‘This would result in a total stoppage of the evolution of factors in a body for millions of years, *as the chain of actions and reactions may not follow the pattern on creation and laws of prakriti*.’ I was wondering if you can explain the part in bold. I thought the problem is that the different components of the body, which need to evolve, would be stuck. Is this the law of prakriti that would not be followed?

The karapatram mentions ‘Unit consciousness may have to continue this journey for an infinite period … .' Does this indicate that some jivas will never get moksha, continuing in the cycle of life and death for infinite time?





**Full Answer:**  
After death, the unit mind is present in seed form only. There is no functionality.

Once the brain is dead, there is zero chance of recovery and hence no point in continuing life support. The mind has no functionality without the brain.

What you are thinking the problem is is indeed exactly that, expressed in a slightly different way. The chain of action and reaction needed for the evolution of those anujiivas is held up so that their evolution is delayed.

When you ask a question kindly mention to which karapatram you refer so it will be clear-- otherwise I have to search around to find out which one you are referring to. Theoretically speaking it is possible. Although the reality is that jiivas ultimately learn from their mistakes-- some more quickly and some in a far more delayed fashion. So the sense is that for some it may take a very, very long time indeed. And although theoretically if they do not learn it could go on endlessly, with a jiiva continuing to degenerate itself every time it gets a human birth. But in reality it is the nature of jiivas to learn, and so ultimately even the slowest learners will get the point and ultimately embark on the journey from 10:00 to 12:00.
