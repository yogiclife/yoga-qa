**Original Question:**  
Clarification by acharya ji.

**Full Answer:**  
X ji's question was very good: What is the difference between the state of "mindlessness" after death, and the state of "mindlessness" in meditative state? What you replied to her--that the mind can only do meditation when it is housed in a body--is very true. I would just add that the former state of "mindlessness"--that which occurs after death--is mundane in the sense that it occurs in the lives of all at the time of death and hence in no way indicates an elevated state. The crudest of human beings when they die go into the state of mind being inactive while they wait to be assigned a new body; they could be waiting to get the body of a mouse because that is what they degenerated themselves to. Whereas the "mindlessness" of meditation is an elevated state. Yadrshii bhavana yasya siddhirbhavati tadrshii. This spiritual elevation happens in meditation only, not after death. So it is not only that one needs a body to do sadhana; that is certainly true. But the "mindlessness" of sadhana is a highly elevated state (due to ideation on PP and becoming suspended in that bhava), whereas the state after death of the general human being is merely a non-functionality of mind due to its being without a body.
