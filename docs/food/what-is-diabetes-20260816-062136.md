**Full Answer:**  
So let us take this in a stepwise fashion:

- There are two types of diabetes: type 1 and type 2. 

- Type 1 is generally childhood onset, in which the cells which produce insulin (the best islet cells in the pancreas) get destroyed by an auto-immune reaction, most likely related to consumption of animal products. Once this happens, that child's body will be unable to produce insulin for the rest of their life. So they will have diabetes for their whole life, and their condition is incurable. That is to say, they will be needing insulin injections for their whole life. But by eating a WFPB diet, they can reduce their insulin needs and stabilize their diabetes dramatically and thereby lead a very healthy life. Type 1 diabetes is a small minority of the cases of diabetes in the world. According to most data, only around 5-10% of people with diabetes have Type 1 diabetes, meaning the vast majority (90-95%) have Type 2 diabetes. 

- Type 2 is a condition of insulin resistance. The body's beta islet cells in the pancreas work fine, and they produce insulin as they are suppose to do. But the insulin is unable to do its work properly as the body has become resistant to it. This condition is in the great majority of cases due to being overweight. Because 90-95% of diabetes is type 2, let us focus on this form of diabetes. All the people in your classes who have diabetes, have type 2 diabetes.

And as a result, the dietary recommendations for type 2 diabetes are just with a view toward eating foods that will not cause the blood sugar to become high.
