**Original Question:**  
"Do you have suggestions on the food items we ought to include daily to get all the needed nutrients? If you have any recipes for tasty raw food, that would be great too! I visited Greece last year and had tasty salads there. But the vegetables in the USA taste like plastic, compared with that. So, I wonder if we need to add some condiments to make them taste good."



So there are two main questions

1. Do you have suggestions on the food items we ought to include daily to get all the needed nutrients?

2. If you have any recipes for tasty raw food, that would be great too!

**Full Answer:**  
Regarding #1: Those who are not vegetarians are the ones who are at risk of all sorts of nutritional deficiencies. Plants have any amazing array of nutrients in them, literally thousands of health-supporting vitamins, minerals, fiber, anti-oxidants, phytochemicals. Animal products contain almost none of any of these things. So people who eat mostly junk food, processed foods, and animal products become deficient in all sorts of things as a result of which they do not have protection against cancer, cardiovascular disease, and many other conditions. Those who eat a pure, whole-food, plant-based diet will be missing only one nutrient: vitamin B12. That one will not dependably get from any food of plant origin, so I recommend taking a vegan vitamin B12 supplement. The bottom line here is that when eating a whole-food, plant-based diet, one need not be concerned about getting all the needed nutrients; those not eating this diet are the ones who need to be concerned :-).



Regarding #2. As you might have sensed, I tend to eat very simply, just raw whole fruits and vegetables. But I do get your point about the tasteless nature of veggies and fruits in US grocery stores. For that reason I eat all organic fruits and vegetables, and those are far better. Although even there, it is best if one can get locally grown organic produce. And in the summer I do that-- i.e. I get my produce from local organic farmers. Those fruits and vegetables are wonderful.



So my approach to food is very, very simple: raw, organic fruits and vegetables--locally grown when available.



But for those who like to make things, there are lots of websites that provide great recipes. Just Google "raw food vegan recipes" and you'll find lots of options. Here is one good one I found just now: https://www.therawchef.com/raw-food-recipes/



When you use recipes from the web, you'll just need to remove the onion, garlic, and mushroom from their recipes, as these three items are tamasik. But there are lots of good alternatives to those ingredients which you can exchange for them.
