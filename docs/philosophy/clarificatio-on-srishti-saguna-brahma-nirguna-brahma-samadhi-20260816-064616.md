**Full Answer:**  
There is quite a variety of types and states of samadhi, accompanied by varying levels of awareness and varying degrees of recollection afterward, along with varieties of post-samadhi experiences. If it is one of the forms of spiritual samadhi, one will surely experience exaltation and bliss afterward, irrespective of the degree of recollection one has of the actual samadhi. Going forward we will learn more about these various experiences, and will also begin to experience them ourselves.





Parama Purusa is greater than the sum of His parts-- He is not merely a conglomerate of unit consciousnesses (whether in potential or manifest form). The reason it is presented that way in the first karapatram is that we need to move ahead stepwise. We are in the process of introducing people to an entirely new view of the universe and of spirituality. People have an array of misconceptions about God and the universe, and the first karapatram is giving all our learners a common base of what dharma is, what mind and unit consciousness are, and what Parama Purusa is. If they understand and incorporate all that is taught in the first karapatram, that is a huge gain, a mammoth accomplishment in view of the outlook they have been maintaining in their lives up till then. The conception of Parama Purusa as the aggregate of all the unit consciousnesses achieves an outlook in which learners come to understand that by any name, Parama Purusa cannot have a form. The understand that their own unit consciousness can have no form, and so the aggregate of all unit consciousnesses must also not have a form. This gives rise to a whole new understanding and vision. And going forward, the main thing to keep in mind is that Parama Purusa is not like a machine which is made of parts. Brahma is One-- including Nirguna Brahma, Saguna Brahma, and Taraka Brahma. And He has a centre, from which He manages everything. That centre is far, far more than the mere addition of potential or manifest unit consciousnesses. There is much more to be said about this, and we will surely do so as we move ahead together.



 The mind can also be divided into conscious, subconscious, and unconscious aspects or parts. Although the conscious mind has direct access only to information it gains via the indriyas, the unconscious mind is linked with the vast consciousness and sadhakas may gain access to whole areas of knowledge (past lives, intuitionally gained knowledge etc). Young children (under 5) may also have access to some parts of their mind which they lose access to later on, and those having near-death experiences may also similarly get temporary access to parts of their mind which they did not have access to in their daily lives.



The srsti is within Saguna Brahma, not the reverse. The entire srsti is occurring within the citta of the Cosmic Mind. Although it has always been in the process of being created and will never end, but it is not considered anadi or ananta as all of its parts have a beginning and an end. This is in stark contrast to Brahma.
