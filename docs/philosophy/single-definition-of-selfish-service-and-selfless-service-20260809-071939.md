**Original Question:**  
Acharya ji, you said about niswarth sewa but what is actually swarth . Is there any single definition for swarth or it is composed of various forms?.

**Full Answer:**  
Good question: So, the meaning of स्वार्थ is self-interest, doing something for oneself. If one performs an act of "सेवा" which is guided or inspired by स्वार्थ, it means one is doing the act with the intention of getting something in return. For example, recognition (by others that one has done something great); or gratitude (on the part of the person for whom one rendered the सेवा); or indebtedness/आभार ( the feeling in the person for whom the service was rendered that they should do something for one in the future). So if a person does seva with aspiration to get any of the above three things in return, then this is not seva but business. One gave something, and one expects something in return. So it became a transactional enterprise, and is not at all seva.



Whereas निःस्वार्थ सेवा is एकतरफ़ा. One did something, and one expects nothing in return. This is true service, true seva. Selfless service is the only seva. One is doing something with the pure intention to help others. One desires neither recognition, nor gratitude, nor compensation of any kind. And the inner bhava behind the act is that I am serving Parama Purusa in the form of this child or adult or animal or plant.



Question again as a follow up: 

But acharya ji , even when a mother feeds her baby child , she have an intention that when the baby will grow up it will feed her . So at that baser instinct also there is swarth, so how can then it be that someone is doing something and is not wanting anything in return. Because motivation of doing something starts with something. If there is not something then why will I work. So I would request you to elaborate how this niswarth sewa can be implemented in our life .



Response as follow up:

If a mother is raising children with the goal/purpose/intention that they will take care of her when she is old, then there is no seva bhava in this and it is an action of स्वार्थ only. The proper approach for a mother is to feed her baby seeing the baby as a manifestation of Parama Purusa, and to feel gratitude that she has the opportunity to serve the baby. 



It is true what you say, that motivation to do something starts with something. But that "something" does not have to be based in personal gain; it can be based in genuine seva bhava. Seva bhava is something which humans are capable of. Animals do not understand seva bhava, although they can be taught to help others. A dog can be taught to save a drowning person, for example. But animals do not have a sufficiently developed mind to generate seva bhava within. Whereas human beings do have this capability. The best way to generate a feeling of seva bhava is to engage in sadhana. Because through sadhana one develops a feeling of love for Parama Purusa and by extension, all that He has made. Because the entire creation is within Him. So if one loves Parama Purusa then one will want to serve Him. And service to His creation is a form of service to Him. This seva bhava, the desire to perform truly selfless service, is something which all human beings are capable of. The seed of this seva bhava lies in sadhana, because seva is rooted in a link with Parama Purusa. Ultimately one develops a feeling of love for Parama Purusa which is so strong that one completely forgets oneself, and everything which one does in life is for Him only.
