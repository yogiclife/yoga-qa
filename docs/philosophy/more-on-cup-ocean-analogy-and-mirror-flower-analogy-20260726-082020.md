**Original Question:**  
cup-ocean analogy and mirror-flower analogy

**Full Answer:**  
Of the two models (cup-ocean analogy and mirror-flower analogy), Brinda ji said the mirror is the more realistic one. As we know, both are extremely helpful; if one has to select a more "realistic" one though, I would say it is the cup in the ocean model. Because the mirror analogy has a weakness that the image in the mirror is a virtual image i.e. has no actual existence, whereas the jiivatma is real and is not a mere reflection. Many can get mislead by this, or develop a misunderstanding from the mirror model that the jiivatma is not real. And of course that aspect of the model is not what the analogy is used for, and so those who correctly understand the analogy will never be mislead. But even then, one needs to understand that the fact that a reflected image is not real does not imply that jiivatma is not real or is merely a reflection. The phenomenon of the reflection is used very specifically to show that, just as if a mirror is clean then the image will be clearly reflected, in the same way if the mind is shuddha and properly developed from following the yoga-nisth jiivan then the jiivatma within will be clearly perceived. But the tendency to mislead about the reality value of the jiivatma remains, for those who do not fully understand the very specific and limited intention of the analogy. 



In contrast, the water in the cup is real water and that real water is representing the jiivatma; so here in this model there is no question of getting misled to think that the jiivatma is not real as the water in the cup is very real. It is very easy to understand that the water in the cup is real water and is the same as the water outside the cup. So this gives an extremely genuine, realistic image of what is actually going on. Remove the cup through sadhana, and the water inside the cup merges with the water outside the cup.



It is true that an analogy always has a specific intent for which it is made, and along with that, due to the fact that it is not the actual situation itself it has certain limitations. But that not withstanding, the fewer limitations it has the better it is and the more readily it lends itself to a proper understanding of the situation it is intended to represent.
