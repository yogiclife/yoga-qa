**Original Question:**  
Automation, how to read, vedanata

**Full Answer:**  
A few points--



1. Automation: There is one sister in your class who has keen interest and asks a lot of questions. She wanted to know when a particular activity gets automated-- such as cutting vegetables or driving a car -- then what part of the human being is in charge of overseeingit? The answer is that the human being has within a janmagata ability to delagate tasks at different levels of control throughout the body. Delegation of tasks: Just as the Pradhan Mantrii of the country is the overall authority and in charge of everything, but doesn't directy oversee what is done at the local post office in some village. There are sub-authorities as well, and sub-sub authorities. So it is in the body. For example, you touched a hot pot with your hand, and the hand jerked away from the pot before the mind even realized what happened. This is called a reflex arc and occurs at the level of the spinal cord. The nerves in the hand send a message to the spinal cord which reflexes a message back to the hand to move quickly away from the pot. The message arrives to the brain and mind only after the hand has already jerked itself away from the pot. Here the work of overseeing has been delegated to the spinal cord. Playing the piano-- finger-movement is automated. So many actions are automated. When we go running, do we consciously move each leg with each step? No. We have trained the legs to do that. In each of these cases, the duty of overseeing has been delegated to a lower level of the nervous system-- often in the spinal cord or at some other level of the nervous system. And what you have said is also correct, that in some cases the ahamtattva and citta are themselves involved, but less often. For example the person is driving a car and also having a conversation: oversee of most of the activities of the hands and feet has been delegated to lower levels of the nervous system, with intermittent checkin in done directly by the ahamtattva and citta as well.



2. At 50:00 you instructed one bhagini to "Go line by line" as she gave her explanation of the para she had read. But you need to explain what you mean by this (Go line by line) and also you need to enforce it. You told bhagini several times to do this, and she never did it. She just gave some brief summary in one or two sentences. So what does it mean to "go line by line"? Tell them: Read aloud the first line of the para, then explain it. Then read aloud the second line, then explain it. Don't let them take an escapist approach of just giving what they understand as a two-sentence summary and then say, "Bhagini this is all I understood". No, no-- guide her what to do and insist that she follow this approach. You are training them in how to go about understanding the text. Rather than just give up and say they don't understand, if they go line-by-line then they will catch the meaning. And together with that, by that approach they will understand what is new in the para, and won't just think it is "repetition of the previous para".



3. Last week you taught them that the word to use is "Brahma", not "Brahman", but during this next session you used the word "Brahman" several times. Take care on this point-- the word to use is "Brahma". 



4. The sister who has keen interest again asked about vedanta in this session. She asked whether the explanation given here of the three part of the mind, and then the jiivatma which is separate from the mind -- whether this explanation is similar to that given in vedanta. There is no need for you to say whether or not you are familiar with what is given in vedanta o this point. Simply say that "We are here to learn what yoga is, what the yogic life is, and to implement and experience it in our life. We are not here to engage in comparative philosophy -- that is done by pustakiiya jinaniis. We are not going to do that-- human life is short!" -- Welcome to use whatever words you like, but this is the basic message. We have more important things to do and learn-- namely, implementation the yogic life: the path that leads to merger with Parama Purusa. (Nobody ever got moksa by studying comparative philosophy.)
