**Original Question:**  
Since saguna brahma has an I feeling, I take it that it has free will. I guess prakriti does not have a free will, because it is not a conscious entity.

I was thinking a little about unit consciousness and am wondering about the need to postulate its existence. 



We justified the need for unit consciousness by saying that mahattattva has a feeling of “I exist”, so there must be an “I” to motivate that feeling; that entity is the unit consciousness. It does not look like the unit consciousness has any other role. But this role could be filled by mahattattva itself. That is, mahattattva is the “I” and thus generates the feeling of “I” exist. Mahattattva also reflects consciousness. But it does not need a unit consciousness to accomplish this. It can directly reflect the infinite consciousness. 



Do we accept unit consciousness because it has been realized by yogis, or is there a need for it in our theory which requires us to accept its existence. I am unable to see the need for it.  It looks like there would be laghavam without jeevatma.



I was also thinking about brahma, which we defined as the collective for purusha and prakriti. I am wondering if this is an explanation for ease of understanding or whether there really are distinct purusha and prakriti. Let me give an analogy to explain my question. 



Let us say there is a point in space. To ease our understanding, we may create an x and y coordinate and say the point is at x = 2 and y = 3. But the actual reality is the point. Representing it as (2, 3) just gives as a concrete understanding of the point. The point is not really composed of the x and y coordinate. 



Similarly, when we say that fire has a burning property, I take it that this separation into a substrate (fire) and guna (burning property) is done for ease representing knowledge. The reality is a fire with burning property. But separating reality into substrates and gunas helps us describe many entities in terms of fewer concepts. 



I am wondering if similarly, brahma is the real entity and separation into concepts of purusha and prakriti are for the ease of understanding.

**Full Answer:**  


SB as the Master of the universe, most certainly has free will. SB is running the entire universe, the Iiishvara, the Niyantraka. And Prakrti simply does as she has been instructed to do.

I appreciate your sincere and deep thinking on this point Mahodaya. 

Unit consciousness is not only needed however, but also most certainly does exist. 

- "Mahattattva has a feeling of “I exist”, so there must be an “I” to motivate that feeling; that entity is the unit consciousness. It does not look like the unit consciousness has any other role. But this role could be filled by mahattattva itself. That is, mahattattva is the “I” and thus generates the feeling of “I” exist."



The "I" behind "I exist" is purusa, not mind. Purusa and mind are two different things. The shuddha "I", unqualified by Prakrti, is consciousness itself. The shuddha "I" cannot be mahattattva as mahattattva is qualified. 



- "Mahattattva also reflects consciousness. But it does not need a unit consciousness to accomplish this. It can directly reflect the infinite consciousness."



Mahattattva reflects the infinite consciousness, yes. And the reflection is the unit consciousness. This is understood by the analogy of a flower surrounded by 100 mirrors. The flower is Paramatma, and the reflection in each mirror is jiivatma. The mirror is unit mind, specifically, its mahattattva.



And the analogy which you often refer to in your classes is also very pertinent here. There is the ocean of consciousness, and a cup filled with ocean water is floating on the ocean. So the ocean water in the cup is jiivatma (unit consciousness), and the ocean surrounding it all around is Paramatma (Supreme Consciousness). The unit consciousness accompanies the unit mind, birth after birth, remains always with unit mind. And when ultimately unit mind gets spiritual realization, then the mind (i.e. the cup) disintegrates and the water in the cup merges into the water of the ocean.



- "Do we accept unit consciousness because it has been realized by yogis, or is there a need for it in our theory which requires us to accept its existence. I am unable to see the need for it.  It looks like there would be laghavam without jeevatma."



Unit consciousness is not merely a heuristic device. It exists and is linked with the unit mind, life after life.



The explanation of how unit mind is derived from unit consciousness in the first karapatram is indeed a heuristic tool for teaching the new student. Mind is created only from 12:00 to 3:00. Some portions of the Cosmic Mind are divided into units at 6:00 when the jiiva emerges from ksititattva. And at that time, a portion of Cosmic Consciousness is assigned and linked to the unit mind to accompany it through its travels in Pratisaincara. What I have told you here is for your understanding-- this is not for explaining to the new students. In due time they will become ready to understand it. The process explained in the first karapatram is extremely useful in the path from known to unknown, generating in sequence from the process of seeing a book, the concept of unit mind and jiivatma, and then ultimately Paramatma.



Brahma composed of Purusa and Prakrti eternally linked as one Entity is not a heuristic device-- this is reality. And there is indeed no way to conceive of NB, SB, the srsti, and how it could working, without this. But this is not merely conception at the level of mind, this is the fundamental reality. And the ultimate mystery behind how it is all working is only realized when the sadhaka through their sadhana comes to realize Paramatma and thereby join the party of that Cosmic Magician.

Two points:

1. Sanjiiv Mahodaya asked whether the terms "real" and "unreal" could be used to describe the distinction between shuddha purusa and the srsti. It is not that this is not addressed in the yoga darshana shastra. We use the terms "absolute reality" for nirguna purusa, and "relative reality" for the gunanvita purusa i.e. the srsti. Yoga shastra does not accept the advaetavada claim of Brahma-satyam, jaganmithyA. And actually, neither do the advaetavadiis. They live their lives in this world, eat food, speak to people, etc etc. If they really felt the world was unreal, then they would not take part in it. It is really just their hypocrisy to take part in this world and at the same time to claim it is all fake, false. One does not take part in something which one believes to be "mithyA". 



The yogii lives in the world and acknowledges its reality status as that of relative reality. This world is the Cosmic Svapna of the Cosmic Mind. But we are all living within that Cosmic Svapna. It is not like the dreams we have when we go to sleep at night, and upon awakening realize it was all just a dream. As such, the Cosmic Svapna has a special reality status completely distinct from the dreams humans have. No one else sees or experiences our dreams except ourselves; whereas, all of us see the same tree in the field. As such, that tree cannot be denied. And neither can the hunger of a child who is starving. Tell the advaetavadiis to go explain their philosophy to those who do not have food to eat-- that their hunger is just fake. See what they reply. And if the advaetavadiis are not given food for a few days, then just see what these advaetavadiis have to say about their own hunger being "fake". 



The yogii's approach is AtmamokSArthaM jagaddhitAya ca. Why jagat-hita? Because we acknowledge the relative reality of the jagat as well as the importance and significance of that relative reality.
