**Original Question:**  
The first paragraph of ‘What is this world raises a question: “Did the infinite number of unit consciousnesses exist before brahma, or did brahma multiply itself into infinite units and hence is called the multiple of unit consciousnesses?”



I guess the first question considers that unit consciousnesses existed first and then formed a collective that is called brahma. 



Is the second question considering a small brahma initially, which later multiplied itself and became a larger brahma with many unit consciousnesses? I guess this is not considering a situation where a large brahma decided to subdivide itself into small unit consciousnesses because of the word “multiply”.



The second paragraph mentions about humans depending on earth for their origin. I take it that earth refers to the prithivi element, rather than planet Earth. 



We saw that the earth element is required for unit consciousnesses to be separated. So, the mind would be formed only when evolution reaches the stage of prithivi. Air, water, etc are still purusha, but the unit consciousnesses has not manifested itself separately with a mind. 



So, am I right that we would not call water and fire as jiva, but would call stone, plants, etc as jiva?



In a human, there are many unit consciousnesses present but only one experiences spiritual evolution through that body. I assume a similar situation arises in animals. 



What happens with inanimate entities like stone? We can break a stone into two pieces. Each should have a unit consciousness that experiences the stone as its body. Will a dormant unit consciousness in the stone get linked to the new piece of stone? I guess a similar situation arises when cells split or microorganisms reproduce through division.

 The third paragraph mentions that humans have originated from the earth and so must have obtained their unit consciousness from the earth. I guess earth here is the planet, because this paragraph later mentions earth arising from the sun. 



Here, I take it that ‘human’ refers to the body, because the mind itself does not appear to arise from the earth. Does the unit consciousness here refer to the jeevatma that is temporarily linked with the body? 



The argument is not clear to me. The body clearly arises from the earth. But the unit consciousness takes different bodies and is only temporarily linked to any body. So, how are we connecting unit consciousness to the earth?
