**Original Question:**  
Why we teach about Qualitative and Quantitative difference?

**Full Answer:**  
For that reason, and also simply to ensure the reason for presenting it is clear, all teachers should abide by the following guideline: Any time we discuss qualitative and quantitative difference, we MUST include prominent mention of the reason for talking about this. The entire reason for teaching these two principles is to explain that qualitative difference justifies jati-bheda whereas quantitative difference does not. To mention these two principles without this sentence really defeats the purpose of teaching it. We are teaching these two terms only to explain that intelligence is not sufficient for justifying jati-bheda between pashu and manushy, as intelligence is merely a quantitative difference. Many people think of humans as the most intelligent animals, and if that is the only difference that exists between manushy and pashu then indeed such people are right-- manushy is just an intelligent pashu. So something more is needed to justify jati-bheda, in the absence of which manushy remains an animal and there is no justification for jati-bheda. This is the entire point. Let me know if this is not clear.
