**Original Question:**  
Clarification by acharya ji.

**Full Answer:**  
All should try to practice upavasa and the breaking/cleaning process the following day. I look forward to hearing how it went. This is a part of the yogic life, which is to say it is part of what it is to lead a truly human life. Just like we all bathe every day. Some children may not want to bathe in the winter especially in India when it is cold outside, but it is the parents' duty to teach them that we have to wash and clean our body every day. In the same way, we have to clean our digestive tract twice per month. That is what the shaoca following upavasa is all about. And the day of upavasa itself is for cultivating a feeling of vaeragya in the life. That is, we live in this world but our minds are not colored by it. The mind remains in the thought of Parama Purusha, the Infinite Entity. This day when the mind is freed from engagement with food, it becomes particularly easy to spend the day aloof from the attractions of this world. So all should try -- you will get success! Instructions for upavasa as well as how to break the next morning and what to eat afterward are all provided in the form of pdfs on our website page The Yogic Life, just beneath the sound files.
