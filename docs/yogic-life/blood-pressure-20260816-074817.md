If someone had had normal kidney function, their numbers would not have shot up like that due to getting dehydrated. He sustained what is called "acute kidney injury" (AKI), and it happened because his kidneys were compromised before he did the climbing. 



The second point is that his doctors do not know much about nutrition. And that is of course not surprising, as doctors are not taught nutrition. The only doctors who know about nutrition are those who educate themselves about it after completing medical school. Your friend's doctors did not do that. The point here is that no fruits or raw vegetables put a strain on the kidneys. The reason his kidneys were strained from the before even going to the  Velliangiri hills was that he had a poor diet for years and years all along, and that resulted in both high blood pressure and renal insufficiency. And that is what put him at risk for AKI in the first place. I don't know whether he used to eat meat and fish, or if not, whether it was due to eggs and dairy products. But it was due to the wrong foods that he developed kidney insufficiency which put him at risk for AKI. Dehydration no doubt tipped him over the edge, but a healthy person would not have gotten tipped over the edge like that due to dehydration; a healthy person would have simply gotten dehydrated but would not have developed AKI.



And the final point is that his doctors are still misguiding him today, telling him to avoid raw fruits and vegetables, when these are the best things he could possibly eat. If there is anything that could help his kidneys it is raw fruits and vegetables. If he were to start eating a diet exclusively of raw fruits and vegetables, his kidneys would be beautifully supported. So he should absolutely switch over to raw fruits and vegetables. That is the way for him to go! By so doing he can cure his hypertension, and his kidney function will also improve.
