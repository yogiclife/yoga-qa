**Original Question:**  
how to explain niirjalaupavasa to someone new

**Full Answer:**  
Namaskar to All and Welcome to our Yogic Life Classes!



It is indeed wonderful to have all of you here with us. Starting to learn something new which is of so much value in the human life, it is a very inspiring time.



My name is Swarup, I am the administrator of our Samskrta website where you may have been receiving announcements from me over the years. I am also a medical doctor, MBBS MD, as well as yogacarya.



I see that some of you have questions regarding nirjalopavasa, and it will be my pleasure to respond to those.



First, may I respond to questions regarding the value and role of modern science in evaluating yogic practices. 



Actually, modern science and yoga work together, hand in hand. The universe as described by modern science, and the history and evolution of life as described by modern science, are both fully supported by yoga. 



There are two limitations to science:

1. It only recognizes objects and phenomena that are indriya-grahya.

2. There are many things in the indriya-grahya world which it has not yet studied. 



Due to #1, science does not recognize the existence of mind or of consciousness (purusa). And due to this shortcoming, there are limits to what science can understand. For example, science is unable to understand the origin of the universe as well as the origin of life -- for both have originated from consciousness, as we will see going forward. Science is also unable to understand anything regarding spirituality, which is a science unto itself.



Due to #2, even among the subjects which are amenable to modern scientific study, there are many subjects which have never been studied. Scientific studies cost money to carry out, and as a result, most scientific studies are carried out in areas in which there is money to be made. For example, there are lots of studies on drugs, because the pharmaceutical industry funds the studies so they can make money from the drugs. Whereas, studies which could really benefit humanity in a deep way are not done, because no one would make any money from them.



And now we come to the subject at hand: nirjalopavasa. Nirjalopavasa would be amenable to study as it is at least in part an indriya-grahya phenomenon. However, because there is no money to be made from such studies, there is a lack of funding and hence an absence of such studies. However, as we say, "Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence." Just because studies have not been done studying nirjalopavasa deeply does not mean it is not a deeply valuable practice, an invaluable component of the yogic life. Studies haven't been done, but they need to be done and in the future they will be done.



Benefits of nirjalopavasa:



1. Safety. There is no question of any danger here. Yogiis have been practicing nirjalopavasa (upavasa with no food or water for 36 hours) for thousands of years with no problem at all. I myself have been practicing nirjalopavasa four (4) times per month for 40 years, and there have not been any negative effects whatsoever. I have furthermore taught this practice to hundreds of people, and none of them have experienced any negative effects. My own BUN/Creatinine as well as GFR (glomerular filtration rate) are perfect, better than anyone else's whom I know. If not taking water for 36 hours were harmful in any way, one would expect that harm to have manifested itself over the course of practicing it four times a month for 40 years. And also in the hundreds of people to whom I have taught it, and who continue to practice it regularly. But no one has ever experienced harm-- rather, they experience huge benefit, which is delineated below.



2. Fasting state: During the upavasa day, if one takes water the body will not go into a fasting state. The "fasting state" is a highly beneficial state in which the body and mind are both positively impacted.

 

(a) The body's metabolic processes all become slowed down, which creates an environment highly conducive to and supportive of healing. 



(b) During the fasting state, the mind gets a feeling of distance from the manifest world, and this feeling is highly conducive to spiritual ideation, spiritual bhava, and deep meditation. On the day of nirjalopavasa the sadhaka can have very blissful, deep sadhana experiences which are generally unobtainable on other days. 



Induction of the fasting state is essential to the spiritual experience of upavasa. "Upavasa" means "living close to Parama Purusa". On the day of upavasa, due to the physiological as well as psycho-spiritual state induced, it is far easier than other days to feel very, very close to Parama Purusa. If one takes water, this does not occur. 



As a practicing physician as well as a yogic practitioner and teacher I can assure you of the safety of nirjalopavasa. The evidence I have is anecdotal to be sure, but is extensive, having been carried out over many decades and in hundreds of people.  



While it is true that the medical studies have not yet been done, thousands of yogiis' experience is testimony to the fact that the practice is completely safe. The medical field carries out thousands of useless studies and doesn't carry out the most needed ones-- nirjalopavasa is not to be blamed for this. It is a jewel for humanity. Up to now this jewel has been kept in the hands of a few only; now it is time to spread its beauty throughout the entire humanity so that all may benefit.



So try it! You will find that there is nothing to be afraid of. Rather, it will change your life dramatically, give you renewed physical vigor as well as a new spiritual anubhava.
