**Original Question:**  
We saw that the mahattattva and ahamtattva face karmaphala. Does the chitta too? It seems that it neither took the decision nor was the cause for it, unlike ahamtattva and mahattattva. We do see that body and senses are affected due to karmaphala. But as I understand, then are not experiencing the karmaphala. The sorrow due to these defects is experienced by the ahamtattva. So, I guess any defect in chitta on account of karmaphala implies that the ahamtattva is experiencing the karmaphala, not the chitta.



**Full Answer:**  
It is the ahamtattva that directly experiences karmaphala, and the mahattattva indirectly. All the karmaphalas take form in the citta itself. It is the citta which gets distorted by actions taken by ahamtattva, and citta which gets brought back to a neutral position on the experiencing of karmaphala. Everything happens at the citta, and is experienced directly by the ahamtattva, indirectly by mahattattva. So the entire mind is involved, in the ways detailed here. Paragraph #7 provides many helpful details on this subject.

That depends on the karmaphala and on the samskaras of the person. All we can say is that mahattattva and ahamtattva both experience the karmaphalas which take form in the citta. What the effects and ramifications will be depend on the particular individual. As a general outlook though, we can say that with the expression of each karmaphala, the samskara load is decreased and by that, the tendency will be for the mind to become lighter and to tend towards elevation. But the response in each individual case may vary dramatically.

So in both the scenarios you described here, it is important to keep in mind that worldly sukha is not better than worldly duhkha -- both are bondage. Bondage is bondage, whether made of chains of gold or iron. So for a sadhaka, there is no desire for acquiring sukha or avoiding of duhkha -- only desire is to please PP, irrespective of whether that brings worldly कष्ट for the sadhaka. And in the example you gave of the person who does charity for gaining fame, they may get worldly suhkha and their mind may also get degenerated by what they did. And the so-called 'atheist' (there isn't really any such thing, if we look deeply into it), who does service with true feeling of compassion, even though done with a feeling of doership, will get samskara during the action but may also, depending on the bhava he has during the action, grow mentally as he does it. He may not yet be able to do true seva as he cannot do the action with desire to please PP, but he may nonetheless benefit from it and it may contribute to his becoming ready to learn sadhana.

If you go carefully through para #7, you'll get the answers to everything you ask here. In that para, see carefully how it is arrived at that "Thus karmaphala is a manifestation of mahattattva." Mind is the doer of the action, and mind experiences the action. So yes the finger depressing the ball is the effect of mahattattva inspiring ahamtattva, which is the entity that in the form of a finger directly depresses the ball, and the ball is the citta, which is also a gunanvita manifestation of mahattattva.

One thing to remember here is that when all the karmaphalas have expressed and the mind is in neutral position regarding its entire history of thousands and thousands of actions, then by the krpa of PP it gets mukti/moksa. Samskara is like a heavy load. It could be a load of gold or it could be a load of mud. When the entire load is gone that means that is the stage of salvation.
