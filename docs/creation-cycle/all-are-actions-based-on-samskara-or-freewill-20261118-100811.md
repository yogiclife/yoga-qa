**Original Question:**  
When we are doing good or bad to someone, is it inspired by our samskara or it’s a new act? How do we know it’s exhausting previous samskars or creating new ones?

**Full Answer:**  
Here is the essence of the question, restated:

1) Are actions a person does always new actions? Or can a person do an action under the inspiration or control of their own samskaras (संस्कार के वश में)? Are events that are prompted by samskaras always events that happen to us, or can they be events that we ourselves do under the वश of samskaras?

2) Are actions we take always new actions that create samskaras (unless done with brahma-bhava), or can actions we take be themselves the reaction to previous actions we ourselves did? 



Here is the answer:

- Are actions a person does always new actions? => Yes.

- Or can a person do an action under the inspiration or control of their own samskaras (संस्कार के वश में)? => No.

- Are events that are prompted by samskaras always events that happen to us? => Yes.

- Or can they be events that we ourselves do under the वश of samskaras? => No.



Now, is it possible to say why this is the case? That is, why is it that samskaras can never be the cause behind what one oneself does? This is a very interesting question.



And here is the answer: What you do, you  have control over that. But when you face reactive momenta then you can't control what happens, you control nothing, you have to face it. 



If we were to say that people do things under the वश of samskara, then people could use this as an excuse and justification for everything they do, saying they are not responsible for it as it was done just due to samskara. A murderer in the courtroom, the thief caught by the police, and all other wrong actions-- people will try to escape responsiblity for these things by saying it was out of their control, just done due to samskara. But it is not the case. Animals take actions based on inborn instincts; human beings take actions based on their conscious decision to do so. Every action a human being takes is based on their premeditated consideration and decision. They think about it, then they do it. This is what we call "free will". Everyone has the option to say something or not say it, do something or not do it. Whatever they say or do they opted to say or do it. They had full freedom to do it or not, and they opted to do it. Without having had the thought take form in their citta, they couldn't have taken the action. 



So to say that people's actions are due to samskara goes 100% against the principle of free will. People are responsible for each and every action they take. Claiming that one's actions are due to samskara relieves them of responsibility for what they did. And that is false and indeed dangerous to the welfare of society. People have free will and are thereby responsibile for what they do; this is an essential quality of what it is to be human, and it is a glorious quality and one that works together with having an infinite desire in allowing them to consciously move towards and ultimately merge in that Infinite Entity, Parama Purusa.
