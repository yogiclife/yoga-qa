**Original Question:**  
Existence of ghost and bodiless mind.

**Full Answer:**  
It is good that you clarified to them that when we say the person who dies becomes a bodiless mind,  this bodiless mind is different from a ghost. As you have mentioned the word "ghost" though, it would be helpful to clarify as well that there is no such thing as a ghost. Many people in India believe in ghosts; the concept of a "ghost" is accepted in Indian society. So for this reason, if you have mentioned that the bodiless mind is not a ghost, it would also be helpful to mention that there is no such thing as a ghost. We have not discussed this subject in our yoga sessions yet, but we will. And for this very reason, that it is important for people's minds to be cleared of all the various sorts of dogmas which occur in the society. "Ghosts" are figments of the imagination. When the mind is in a weak or vulnerable state, what was previously created in the imagination now appears to be real. The same is the case when people say that someone has been possessed by a ghost, god or goddess. It is all imagined by the person involved--nothing more than that.

3. After a person dies and their mind leaves the body, it cannot do any work and so cannot do sadhana or service and therefore cannot continue its journey toward merger in the Supreme Entity until it again  receives a body and takes birth. In the same way that it cannot evolve, it cannot devolve or degenerate either. It is the actions a mind takes in the world which lead to its evolution or degeneration. In the absence of a body the mind cannot take any action and is "frozen" as it were, awaiting placement in a new body. (Just wanted to make sure this is clear as I think I heard you mention that the bodiless mind can devolve.) The mind of a frog may get rebirth in the body of dog in its next life, and the mind of a human may get rebirth in the body of a mosquito in its next life, but these assignments by Prakrti are on the basis of the actions the mind took during its previous life, and its resultant mental state at the time of death.
