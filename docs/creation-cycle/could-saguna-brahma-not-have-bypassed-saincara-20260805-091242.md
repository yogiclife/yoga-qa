**Original Question:**  
Could Saguna Brahma not have "bypassed" saincara?

**Full Answer:**  
When Saguna Brahma ( SB) is all-knowing and all-powerful, could SB not have "bypassed" saincara. 



You replied giving the point about how when a cook makes ksiira it has to be done stepwise, slowly, otherwise ksiira will not be able to be made. And that similarly, for life to form, the 5 FF are needed -- water, fire, air are all needed. And air comes from vyoma, everything happens stepwise.



Raji ji then asked, but the cook is a mere human being and SB is limitless. SB did not need to follow such a limited pathway, but even so SB followed it. Why? When SB is all-powerful, then why is He trapped in having to follow the steps like a cook?



Her follow-up question was appropriate, as your initial answer about the cook made it sound like SB is trapped in the system and has no option but to follow the steps just as a cook does. 



You then replied to her that 5 FF in srsticakra is needed, and we are slowly coming to understand this through yoga and through science. Air and water as well as solid are needed for the creation and for life to exist. And rules are needed. Every day the sun rises in the east and sets in the west, and if these rules are not followed there will be chaos.



Finally, Raji ji asked whether before starting the creation SB had already pre-conceived how it wanted the srsti to be made and how the universe would be, and only then started making creation according to the preconceived plan which was in Its mind? That there will be living beings etc, and they will be needing all these 5 FF to live?



And you supported her on this and said that this is true. So by this, after multiple rounds of back and forth the two of you together moved toward the solution.



So in response to the question as to why, when SB is all-knowing and all-powerful, could SB not have "bypassed" saincara, the full reply is: 



1) SB could have set up any system He liked for the srsti. He had complete freedom in this regard. He need not have created the 5FF, it could have been something very different. But once He decided how He wanted it to be, then naturally He insisted on following the rules which He Himself had set up. This first point is the main point.



2) And then only as a second, subsidiary step one can give the example of the cook. SB is not like the cook in that the cook never had any option-- he has to follow the already-existing system to make ksiira. Whereas SB could have made any system He liked; once He had set it up, naturally He will insist that it be followed: Hence the process of saincara.
