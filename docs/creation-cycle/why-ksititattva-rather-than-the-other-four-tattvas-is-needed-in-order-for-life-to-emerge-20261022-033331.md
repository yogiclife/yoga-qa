**Original Question:**  
Why ksititattva (rather than the other four tattvas) is needed in order for life to emerge ? 

**Full Answer:**  
When you explained why ksititattva (rather than the other four tattvas) is needed in order for life to emerge, you said that only ksititattva can "hold" life. While it is not wrong to say that, it would be more clear to explain it by saying that only ksititattva can maintain a separate existence. We use the examples of water or fire, saying that if two fires are brought in contact they will merge and become one fire, and if two bodies of water are brought in contact they will become one body of water. But if two stones are brought in contact they will still remain separate as two different stones. This separateness is what is essential for being able to support an independent live, an independent jiiva. Because, as we know, just as a stone is not inert, so in the same way water, fire, air, and space are also not inert. They also are made of consciousness, and are vibrant with life. So the reason ksititattva is needed will be more clear if, rather than saying that ksititattva is a "holder" of life, say that ksititattva is capable of maintaining separateness, and as such it can support the establishment of a jiiva that is separate from other jiivas and will not simply merge into the other jiivas if it comes in physical contact with them. A jiiva has to be able to maintain a separate existence; separation is essential. The word "hold" is not wrong, it can also be included-- but the word "separate" is essential here to make it clear.
