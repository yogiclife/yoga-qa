**Original Question:**  
I want to understand the karmaphala experienced by people doing good action for non-spiritual reasons. These would be independent actions. Let us say someone does charity for getting fame. I recall we looked at this before. Since he was motivated by the finite, it still degrades the mind. On the other hand, as karmaphala, would he still get some sukha, since he gave sukha to others? Now let us consider an atheist who helps someone out of compassion. He is still having the feeling of doership, and thus it is an independent action. So, he would experience karmaphala. Does his mind elevate, though, because his action was motivated by compassion rather than self interest?



**Full Answer:**  
One may ask whether karmaphala that bring sukha are more beneficial than karmaphala that bring duhkha. Or someone who understands slightly more may ask, "Regarding these, as we know, both acts result in bondage, one of iron and one of gold. Is the reaction to both the actions -- one a reaction of bringing mental sorrow and the other bringing mental happiness -- are both reactions beneficial to the mind in that, being karmaphala, they bring the mind back to a neutral state? Is one more beneficial than the other regarding spiritual development or do they have the same effect?"



The answer to the above question is that it depends on the free will of that very person. For example if due to good action of the past life a person won the lottery in this life and received a huge amount of money, it may be that he then started harming others with that money. In that case the money that resulted in so much sukha is bringing about his degeneration. Whereas another person who won the lottery may start donating to others for the cause of their development, and so in that person's case the money which resulted in sukha also led to their upliftment. 



And in the case of a karmaphala resulting in duhkha, suppose that a particular person did some bad deeds, and as karmaphala his son died. After that it could be that he started thinking deeply on how this world is temporary, and in result became a sadhaka. Or on the other hand, it could be that he became more dangerous and started doing more crime. So it depends on the free will of that very person. 



Whether the karmaphala results in the mind's elevation or degeneration depends on how the person responds to the karmaphala, rather than on whether the karmaphala results in worldly sukha or duhkha.
