**Original Question:**  
At the time of death if one does not get mukti-moksa then, what happens to the unit mind and jiivatma?

**Full Answer:**  
At the time of death if one does not get mukti-moksa then true, the unit mind and jiivatma coupled together await assignment by Prakrti to a new body. And yes it is true that the first time of becoming a jiiva, moving from 3:00 to 6:00 it is needed to reach to ksititattva so as to be able to reach a state in which the unit can be kept separate so that it can sustain a unit mind. But what I would like to clarify here is that once the unit mind has reached to becoming a human being, at that point having started taking independent actions and having thus generated samskaras, at the time of death those samskaras keep the bodiless unit mind and jiivatma as a distinct unit even in the absence of ksititattva. So even without ksititattva it does stay separate after death due to unexpressed samskaras, and this is the case for animals and plants as well who, although not taking independent actions, do have what are called sahajata samskaras which are essentially the force of the sahajata vrttis implanted in them by Prakrti. And yet after death the mind and jiivatma, although a separate unit, being bodiless cannot do anything. It cannot do sadhana or seva, and so cannot move ahead from 10:00 to 12:00 until it again receives a human body.
