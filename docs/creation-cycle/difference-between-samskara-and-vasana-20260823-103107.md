**Original Question:**  
 Mahodaya, is there a difference between samskara and vasana? We have a desire for pleasure through the finite, arising from our prior animal births, isn’t it? So, there must be an impression on our mind in our animal birth, which causes that tendency. I thought this was vasana. Is this different from samskara? 



In people’s first birth as a human, I assume they will have different tendencies because they had different experiences in their prior plant and animal births. Is this right?

**Full Answer:**  
Samskara and vasana are not all the same thing. Samskara simply means abhukta pratikriya. It can be of such a nature that when it expresses as karmaphala it drags one down into animal life, or alternatively, it may be of such a nature that it awakens the desire for the infinite within and inspires one to seek the true human life.



If someone has good samskaras they will engage deeply in the yogic life and progress rapidly in their movement toward merger with Parama Purusa.

Regarding a person's first birth as a human, I am looking into more detail on just how it is that their previous animal lives affect their state of mind.
